# Email System Audit

Email problems rarely come from one bad habit. They come from a system nobody designed: notifications on for everything, dozens of newsletters, folders nobody uses, colleagues copying everyone, no rule for response times and an inbox checked fifty times a day. Each part costs a few minutes; together they cost hours a week and much of the person's focus.

This skill audits how a person or small team uses email, measures where time goes, finds the few changes with the biggest effect and builds a simple email system: rules, filters, a triage rhythm, response targets, templates and team norms. It is the entry point for the Email and Inbox set.

## Operating principles
1. **Measure before changing.** Volume, sources and time spent show where the real problem sits.
2. **Fix the source, not the symptom.** Unsubscribing and team norms beat faster reading.
3. **Few moving parts.** A handful of categories, filters and routines the person will keep.
4. **Protect focus.** Email should fit into set sessions, not interrupt every hour.
5. **Keep people informed.** Clear response targets make batching safe.

## Audit areas
| Area | What to check | Typical finding |
|---|---|---|
| Volume and sources | Emails a day by type: people, notifications, newsletters, internal lists | Over half is automated or bulk mail |
| Checking habits | How often the inbox is opened; notifications on or off | Checked constantly; alerts for every message |
| Triage | How messages get decisions | Messages read several times before action |
| Structure | Folders, labels, filters | Many folders, few used; no filters |
| Response times | Expectations by sender type | No targets; anxiety about slow replies |
| Writing | Length and clarity of sent emails | Long emails, unclear asks |
| Follow-ups | How promises and waiting items are tracked | Relies on memory; things slip |
| Templates | Repeated replies | Same answers rewritten weekly |
| Team norms | Cc habits, reply-all, chat versus email | Everyone copied; urgent issues by email |
| Security | Phishing awareness, two-step login | Payment change requests not verified |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it when email takes too much of the day, the inbox feels out of control, a person starts a new role, a team wants better email habits, or before setting up an AI email assistant.

Don't use it to sort today's inbox (`inbox-triage`) or to write a specific reply (`email-reply-drafting`).

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Role and the people the person most needs to hear from.
2. Rough daily volume, or access to count a typical week.
3. Current folders, labels and filters.
4. Notification settings on desktop and phone.
5. What feels most broken.
6. For teams: how email, chat and meetings are used.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Count a typical week
Group messages by source: named people, internal lists, notifications from tools, newsletters and promotions, external requests. Note which sources lead to action.

### Step 2. Estimate time
Time in the inbox per day, how many sessions, and time on replies versus reading.

### Step 3. Score each area
Score 0 to 3 with one line of evidence each.

### Step 4. Find the top leaks
Usually: too much bulk mail, constant checking, no follow-up tracking, or team habits that create mail.

### Step 5. Design the system
- Triage two to four times a day with the six triage categories.
- Filters that move bulk mail and notifications out of the main inbox.
- Response targets by sender type.
- A waiting-for list for follow-ups.
- Templates for the five most repeated replies.
- Notifications off except for priority senders.

### Step 6. Agree team norms (if relevant)
When to use email versus chat versus a call, Cc and reply-all rules, subject line conventions, and the route for urgent issues.

### Step 7. Roll out gradually
Week one: filters and unsubscribing. Week two: triage rhythm and notifications. Week three: templates and follow-up tracking. Week four: team norms.

### Step 8. Measure again
After a month, compare volume, time in the inbox and missed follow-ups.

## Scorecard template
```
| Area | Score (0-3) | Evidence | Fix | Skill |
| Volume | 1 | 62% bulk mail | Unsubscribe and filter | newsletter-cleanup, inbox-rules-filters |
| Follow-ups | 0 | Two missed client replies | Waiting-for list | follow-up-tracking |
```

## Response targets example
| Sender | Target |
|---|---|
| Key clients and manager | Same working day |
| Team | Within 24 hours |
| External requests | Within 3 working days |
| Newsletters and notifications | No reply needed |

## Team email norms example
- Email for decisions and external contact; chat for quick questions; calls for anything urgent or sensitive.
- Cc only people who need to act or must know; say why in the first line.
- Reply-all only when everyone needs the answer.
- Subject lines start with the action and date when one is needed.
- No expectation of replies outside working hours.

## Measures to track
| Measure | Why |
|---|---|
| Emails a day reaching the main inbox | Volume after filters |
| Inbox sessions a day and minutes per session | Checking habits |
| Median reply time to priority senders | Responsiveness |
| Missed follow-ups a month | Reliability |
| Bulk senders unsubscribed | Source reduction |
Record a baseline before changes and compare after four weeks.

## Quick wins in the first hour
Turn off new-mail notifications, unsubscribe from the ten noisiest senders, create one filter for tool notifications, and book three daily triage slots in the calendar.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Role is mostly email (support, sales) | Shorter triage cycles; strong templates; shared inbox rules |
| Executive with assistant | Delegation rules and a daily brief |
| Heavy regulatory record-keeping | Keep archiving and retention rules; never auto-delete |
| Several accounts | One triage session covering all, or forwarding to one inbox |
| Team resists norms | Trial for four weeks and review the results together |

## Worked example
A sales manager received about 180 emails a day and spent close to three hours in the inbox. The audit found 58% bulk and tool notifications, the inbox open almost all day, no filters and two missed client follow-ups in a month. The plan unsubscribed from 40 senders, filtered tool notifications into a weekly digest folder, set three triage sessions, turned off notifications except for key clients, added a waiting-for list and five reply templates, and agreed Cc rules with the team. A month later, daily volume in the main inbox had fallen by more than half, inbox time was about an hour, and no client follow-ups were missed.

```
## Team norms (if relevant)
## Four-week rollout
## Measures to check after a month
```

## Common mistakes
- Reading faster instead of reducing volume.
- Folders for everything.
- Notifications on for all mail.
- No response targets, so batching feels risky.
- Ignoring team habits that create mail.

## Self-check before you answer
- Is volume measured by source?
- Are the top two or three leaks fixed first?
- Is the system simple enough to keep?
- Is there a measure to check after a month?
